## 1. Executive Summary & Attribution Map

The primary goal of RCIR v8.1 ablations is to understand **where performance gains and losses originate** when moving from unstructured BFS (v7) through early-truncated graph traversal (v8 initial) to the Dual-Plane architecture (v8.1).

### The Three Structural Generations

| Generation | Policy Label | Candidate Pool Recall | Silent Misses | Precision@20 | Precision@50 | Graded nDCG@50 | MRR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **RCIR v7** | Baseline P0 | **98.61%** | 10 | 10.00% | 5.20% | 0.1924 | 0.5059 |
| **RCIR v8 Initial** | Policy P5 | 38.56% | 443 | 36.00% | 22.40% | 0.5425 | **0.7286** |
| **RCIR v8.1 Dual-Plane** | R0 Ranker | **97.23%** | 20 | **46.00%** | **34.00%** | 0.4972 | 0.6733 |
| **RCIR v8.1 Dual-Plane** | R5 Multi-factor | **97.23%** | 20 | 29.00% | 18.40% | 0.3718 | 0.6667 |

### Attribution Insights
1. **Candidate Pool Reachability:** Driven by protecting direct exact graph edges and qualifying seed symbols via `EntityRegistry`. Slicing off BFS frontiers with arbitrary `max_candidates` caps is the sole reason v8 dropped 443 dependencies.
2. **Top-20 Precision:** Driven by explicit `entity_match` (100.0 weight) and `boundary_contract` (40.0 weight). Linear features immediately separate the core modification target from background consumers.
3. **Cross-Module Penalty Tradeoff:** Heavy module distance penalties ($P_{\text{module}} = 15.0$) disproportionately punish valid architectural consumers in decoupled micro-apps (such as `apps/files_trashbin` consuming `NodeDeletedEvent`).
